In hindsight, this is how we'd do it

As for the route itself - going from Komodo westward really is a pretty smart route. In hindsight we'd go east to west - meaning land in Komodo (maybe take a slightly fancier boat than we did), and continue, even by ferry (if possible), to Uluwatu, Bali.

From there take a ferry or a short flight to Java (we rented the car in Surabaya, but maybe you can do it elsewhere too) and do the road trip.

In hindsight, from Surabaya we'd drive straight to the sea turtle national park. All the city stops on the way aren't really worth the time and effort, and it's better to get as fast as possible to natural, rural and more remote areas, where the nature is really beautiful and special.

From Java you can move on to Sumatra, like we did. Ending in North Sumatra let us book a flight to Bangkok for ₪300 each.

We kind of regretted starting in Bali, and specifically in Ubud. If Bali, then Uluwatu and not Ubud.

General tips

What we learned about the whole trip, not about one place.

Planning and route

In general, some understanding of the geography of the islands and their order really helps you plan a good trip. There are also more remote islands that are considered very special and worth checking out, and the flights aren't that expensive either. But the sequence we did is pretty much ideal and gave us a really varied trip.

As for the route itself - going from Komodo westward really is a pretty smart route. How we'd do it in hindsight →

Money and budget

We found it easy to remember that 100,000 Indonesian rupiah is ₪17, and do all the math from there.

We withdrew cash only at BNI, and honestly the fees always looked really low. We used a LAYA card with dollars and the fees were totally fine.

Even though Indonesia is cheap, because of all the flights and the fairly special attractions it came out relatively expensive for us, so it's worth understanding that in advance.

Where to stay

It's important to keep a few apps like Agoda, Trip and so on, and always compare prices on Google Maps to find the best price. Accommodation in Indonesia really is good quality and cheap, and it's worth digging into it to get good prices.

In Java hotels shouldn't cost more than ₪100. In Labuan Bajo we're talking nights for about ₪60.

Food

Food on the road in Indonesia isn't easy. Honestly it's worth always bringing bread, peanut butter and all kinds of things that can fill a gap.

There are local restaurants everywhere - extremely cheap. They serve rice, tofu and tempeh, and for the brave, other stuff of course. The problem is that a lot of the time they're pretty dirty and it's not easy to find clean, good places - so take that into account.

On the Komodo boat trip you really need to tell them about vegan and celiac, otherwise they barely pay attention to it.

Getting around and driving

Grab is very convenient and safe. An important, must-have app.

Renting a car in Java - all in all a great idea, and doable. Drive carefully. We paid 625,000 a day including basic insurance.

The whole car thing isn't nearly as dramatic as it looks. We didn't see a single police officer. And risk-wise - yes, you drive on the left and it's sometimes crowded, but if you drive only in daylight and responsibly, there's no real reason anything should happen.

I do recommend a jeep-type car, which gives you more flexibility. And remember you need a card for the toll roads in Java.

Flights

Flying Lion Air - expect delays and plan for them! We had three delays and nobody answered anywhere. Air Asia was excellent.

When a direct flight is expensive, you can split it (we flew from Komodo to Bali and from there to Surabaya).

SIM and WhatsApp

We got a data plan at the airport we landed in - we really recommend Telkomsel. Excellent reception and a great price. We did about 60 GB a month, and if you want to top up you can do it at the convenience stores that are everywhere here.

The recommendation is to get a SIM and open WhatsApp Business, so your number is Indonesian and you don't use your Israeli number. We did that and it was very convenient.

People and safety

Indonesians are insanely nice and you can really trust them.

It's honestly recommended not to say you're from Israel - we met an Indonesian guide who told us it's worth sticking to that. But in terms of safety and trusting people - we were very pleasantly surprised.

Emergencies and offline maps

On the road trip and in general - I recommend saving Indonesia's emergency numbers and downloading the area's maps offline in Google Maps.

Ijen and Bromo

Bromo and Ijen sometimes close because of fires, and unfortunately we ended up missing both.

It's worth staying in touch with local guides and checking their status all the time, for example with AI tools (we checked with Instinct). These are amazing, very touristy sites, and you need to keep track, book tickets in advance and stay sharp, especially during a period of fires and closures.

Bali: Ubud and Uluwatu

“Overall, our feeling is that Uluwatu beats Ubud: a better vibe, the sea, better food.”

In hindsight: less Ubud

We kind of regretted starting in Bali, and specifically in Ubud. Bali is a very touristy place, and it's not a great way to get to know Indonesia and feel like you're really in Indonesia.

If Bali, then Uluwatu and not Ubud. At least in our experience, Ubud isn't really like it looks in the photos: lots of tourists, the food isn't that tasty, not that pretty. If you do go to Ubud - there are links to good things below.

Road trip in East Java

“Our general recommendation is to research a lot on Google Maps and get to more rural, special areas.”

Java overall is really beautiful, even without us doing Bromo and Ijen. Get away from the urban areas and reach villages and national parks, with an emphasis on the sea turtle tour, which is a must.

In hindsight: from Surabaya straight to the turtles

In hindsight, from Surabaya we would have driven straight to the sea turtle national park. All the city stops on the way aren't really worth the time and effort, and it's better to get as fast as possible to natural, rural and more remote areas, where the nature is really beautiful and special.

The hotels and the waterfall on the way are listed below in the order we did them, for anyone who stops anyway.

Tumpak Sewu Waterfall

Nature & sights · Lumajang · 12.9
Arrive before 08:00No guide neededEntry fee

The first real stop of the trip is these massive waterfalls. Honestly it's really, really worth arriving early, before 08:00. We got there at about 08:40 and it was already pretty packed.

We did it without a guide. You just walk around, and there are some caves that let you climb back up on a loop trail. Worth asking people there so it's clear. There's an entry fee, and it's a very impressive place.

Java Turtle Lodge Meru Betiri

Stay · Meru Betiri, Sarongan · 12–14.9
Cheap and worth itPrivate tour: 2 days + 1 nightSea turtles

A really, really, really warm recommendation, cheap and worth it to an unusual degree. From here you go out for two days in a national park and release baby sea turtles, but the hotel itself and the area are just wow. This is real Indonesia, and incredible nature.

The lodge runs a two-day, one-night tour at a very high level!! A private tour, driving through about the most beautiful jungle we've ever been in. You see a sea turtle come up the beach looking for a place to lay her eggs, all with meals and amazing stuff.

So far this area is about the best experience we've had in Indonesia, so don't miss it no matter what! Highly recommended.

6 · Sep 17

West Bali

“Anyway, it's an excellent area with dive clubs and great restaurants.”

We decided to move to West Bali by ferry, mainly because it felt like there weren't enough interesting things in the northern part of Java on the way back to Surabaya. It looked like West Bali has a national park and good, cheap resorts, so we decided to move there after the days at the amazing resort.

About the ferry - on the face of it the most sensible thing is to leave the car (we left it at the resort) and take the ferry on foot. On the other hand, it costs only 450,000 rupiah to cross with a car. We didn't look into that option so I won't go into it, but I think it's totally worth checking, because that flexibility can save both money and time, and also be fun.

We crossed on foot and looked at lots of resorts.

7 · Sep 19–22

North Sumatra: the orangutans

“It's an amazing experience, very authentic and special.”

From Surabaya we moved to Sumatra, to the orangutan reserve. The reserve in North Sumatra is one of the only places in the world where you can still see orangutans in the wild (basically only in Indonesia).

In general our route made sense location-wise: ending in North Sumatra let us book a flight to Bangkok for ₪300 each.

The flight from Surabaya to North Sumatra (the airport in the city of Medan, called KNO) cost about ₪400 each.

Ponidi, jungle guide

Nature & sights · Bukit Lawang · 22.9

We got loads of recommendations for a guide called Ponidi, who loves Israelis.

The good thing about Ponidi is that he organizes everything - airport pickup, a hotel in the village you start the jungle trek from, food, the ride back, etc. So you don't need to worry about anything.

Bukit Lawang

Nature & sights · North Sumatra · 22.9
The jungle starts hereBack by rafting

The village you set out from for the orangutan jungle trek.

On the last day you raft down the river back to the village, and stay one more night at the same hotel before heading back to the airport the next day.

Google Maps ↗

What the trek is like

So basically it's two nights and three days: looking for monkeys, walking in nature, eating well.

I will say it's not the easiest. The conditions are jungle conditions in terms of showers etc., and you walk quite a lot every day, including lots of uphill.

On the last day you raft down the river back to the village of Bukit Lawang, and stay one more night at the same hotel.
